## ABSTRACT

**Background:** We evaluated joint associations of liver elastography (VCTE) and inflammation (hs-CRP) with cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome and compared FIB-4 with VCTE.

**Methods:** In 7,043 adults aged 30–79 (NHANES 2017–2023) with valid VCTE and bridged hs-CRP, joint phenotypes were defined by physical fibrosis (VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa) and inflammation (hs-CRP $\ge 2.0$ mg/L). Survey regressions ($df=40$) evaluated interaction. FIB-4 ($\ge 1.30$) accuracy was stratified by age (<65 vs. $\ge 65$).

**Results:** Weighted prevalence of advanced CKM was 13.8% ($n=1,246$), VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa was 10.5%, and hs-CRP $\ge 2.0$ mg/L was 45.6%. In Model 2, isolated high hs-CRP had aOR 1.26 (95% CI: 1.02–1.55) and isolated high VCTE had aOR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.53–1.35), whereas dual high status conferred aOR 2.01 (95% CI: 1.39–2.92). Multiplicative ($p=0.0185$) and additive synergism were significant: odds-scale RERI 0.91 (95% CI: 0.19–1.63; AP: 0.45); standardized prevalence interaction +5.9 percentage points (95% CI: 1.3–10.5; prevalence-scale RERI: 0.47, AP: 31.0%). Dual high status was associated with chronic kidney disease with aOR 2.36 (95% CI: 1.70–3.28) and clinical CVD with aOR 1.50 (95% CI: 1.01–2.23). Overall, FIB-4 had 34.8% sensitivity and 14.5% PPV for physical fibrosis. In adults $\ge 65$, FIB-4 false-positive rates surged to 63.8% (vs. 14.3% in adults <65).

**Conclusions:** Co-elevated VCTE liver stiffness and hs-CRP are synergistically associated with advanced CKM syndrome. Against the non-invasive VCTE reference threshold (≥8.0 kPa), standard FIB-4 (≥1.30) exhibits marked age-dependent false-positive inflation in older adults that is mitigated at the expense of sensitivity by age-calibrated thresholds (≥2.00).

**Keywords:** Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome; Vibration-Controlled Transient Elastography; FibroScan; High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein; Hepatic Fibrosis; Complex Survey Design.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic (CKM) syndrome reflects interconnected metabolic risk factors, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and cardiovascular disease (CVD) [1–3].

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) affects >30% of adults [4,5]. Hepatic fibrosis accelerates vascular and renal injury via pro-inflammatory cytokines and endothelial dysfunction [6–9]. The Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index combines age, AST, ALT, and platelets [10,11], but its age numerator inflates false positives in older individuals [12,13].

Transient elastography (VCTE; FibroScan) directly measures liver stiffness in kilopascals (kPa), independent of chronological age [14,15]. Concurrently, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) captures vascular inflammation [16,17]. We evaluated multiplicative and additive synergism between liver stiffness (VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa) and inflammation (hs-CRP $\ge 2.0$ mg/L) for advanced CKM (Stages 3–4) in NHANES 2017–2023, comparing VCTE with FIB-4 across age strata ($df = 40$).

## 2. METHODS

### 2.1 Study Design and Population Universe
NHANES is a continuous probability sample of the US civilian population (CDC/NCHS) with written consent and ethics approval. Data were from 2017–March 2020 (`P_`) and 2021–2023 (`_L`). Retaining the demographic universe ($N = 27,493$) preserved design degrees of freedom ($df = 40$) across all 79 stratum–PSU combinations and 39 pseudo-strata, with non-domain participants contributing zero-score influence. The primary complete-case cohort comprised adults aged 30–79 with examination weights, biochemistry panels, and valid VCTE and hs-CRP ($N = 7,043$). The adult core cohort included participants aged $\ge 20$ ($N = 11,585$).

### 2.2 Physical Liver Ultrasound Transient Elastography (VCTE / FibroScan)
Liver stiffness and controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) were measured in the Mobile Examination Center using the FibroScan 502 Touch (Echosens; M/XL probes) [18]. Reliable exams required complete status (`LUAXSTAT == 1`), $\ge 10$ valid measurements (`LUANMVGP >= 10`), stiffness interquartile range-to-median ratio $\le 30.0\%$ (`LUXSIQRM <= 30.0`), and $\ge 3$ hours fasting. Fibrosis was VCTE ≥ 8.0 kPa; steatosis CAP ≥ 274 dB/m [15,19].

### 2.3 Orthogonal Systemic Inflammation (hs-CRP)
Particle-enhanced immunoturbidimetric assays transitioned from Roche Cobas 6000 to 8000. Forward bridging for 2017–2020 `LBXHSCRP` was:
$$\text{hs-CRP}_{\text{bridged}} = \max(0.08, -0.08653 + 0.9672 \times \text{LBXHSCRP})$$
2021–2023 values were unadjusted. High hs-CRP was defined as $\ge 2.0$ mg/L [16,17].

### 2.4 Joint Phenotype Classification
At thresholds of VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa and hs-CRP $\ge 2.0$ mg/L, Group 0 had neither elevation, Group 1 isolated hs-CRP elevation, Group 2 isolated VCTE elevation, and Group 3 both elevations.

### 2.5 Primary and Secondary Clinical Outcomes
Advanced CKM comprised Stages 3–4 [1,2]. Stage 3 required very-high-risk CKD or PREVENT 10-year CVD risk ≥20%. Kidney filtration used race-free 2021 CKD-EPI equations. Stage 4 comprised reported CVD. Stages 0–2 were non-advanced: no risk factors; adiposity (BMI ≥25 kg/m² or waist ≥88/102 cm in women/men); or hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, or moderate CKD.

Secondary endpoints included overt clinical cardiovascular disease, moderate-to-severe chronic kidney disease (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m² or UACR ≥30 mg/g), and KDIGO very-high-risk chronic kidney disease, alongside secondary validation in the extended adult core cohort aged ≥20 years.

### 2.6 Statistical and Epidemiological Methods
Analyses used strata, clusters, and duration weights: 3.2/5.2 × `WTMECPRP` and 2.0/5.2 × `WTPH2YR`.

Model 1 adjusted for age (per 10 years), sex, race/ethnicity, and cycle. Co-primary Model 2 added poverty-income ratio (PIR), education, BMI (per 5 kg/m²), and waist (per 10 cm). Model 3 added HbA1c, systolic pressure (per 10 mm Hg), non-HDL cholesterol (per 50 mg/dL), log-triglycerides, antihypertensive and hypoglycemic medications.

Multiplicative interaction was tested via product terms. Additive interaction used RERI = OR₁₁ − OR₁₀ − OR₀₁ + 1, AP = RERI/OR₁₁, and S = (OR₁₁ − 1)/(OR₁₀ + OR₀₁ − 2) with Taylor linearization influence functions (df = 40) [20,21]. Because odds ratios overestimate prevalence ratios when outcomes are common, we also estimated marginal survey-standardized prevalences via predictive margins and computed the additive interaction contrast on the probability scale (percentage points), alongside prevalence-scale $\text{RERI}_{\text{PR}}$ and $\text{AP}_{\text{PR}}$, using delta-method Taylor series linearization influence functions (df = 40).

FIB-4 ≥1.30 [22] was evaluated against the non-invasive physical VCTE reference threshold (≥8.0 kPa). Survey-weighted sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative predictive values (PPV/NPV), likelihood ratios (+LR/−LR), and false-positive rates (FPR = 1 − specificity) were calculated overall and by age (<65/≥65). Age calibration used FIB-4 ≥2.00 at ≥65. Multivariable CKM discrimination used survey C-statistics. Python 3.11 analyses used statsmodels, scipy, patsy, and pandas with automated verification.

## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1 Population Characteristics and Baseline Phenotypes
Of 7,756 eligible participants, 7,052 (90.9%) had reliable VCTE (≥10 readings; IQR/M ≤30.0%), and 7,043 had valid hs-CRP, representing 121.7 million US adults aged 30–79 (**Table 1**).

Advanced CKM prevalence was 13.8% (95% CI: 12.5%–15.1%; $n = 1,246$ unweighted events). Prevalence of elevated stiffness (VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa) was 10.5% (95% CI: 9.6%–11.5%), and high systemic inflammation (hs-CRP $\ge 2.0$ mg/L) was 45.6% (95% CI: 43.4%–47.9%).

Joint phenotypes were:
- **Group 0 (Low VCTE / Low hs-CRP):** 50.6% weighted ($n = 3,416$)
- **Group 1 (Low VCTE / High hs-CRP):** 39.0% weighted ($n = 2,810$)
- **Group 2 (High VCTE / Low hs-CRP):** 3.8% weighted ($n = 283$)
- **Group 3 (Dual High VCTE + hs-CRP):** 6.7% weighted ($n = 534$)

As shown in **Table 1**, Group 3 had mean BMI 38.1 kg/m² (95% CI: 37.1–39.1), mean waist circumference was 121.6 cm (95% CI: 119.4–123.8), mean HbA1c was 6.5% (95% CI: 6.3%–6.6%), and prevalence of diabetes mellitus was 43.1% (95% CI: 36.8%–49.7%). Advanced CKM prevalence was 11.0% (95% CI: 9.9%–12.2%) in Group 0 and 14.8% (95% CI: 12.6%–17.3%) in Group 1, 16.2% (95% CI: 11.9%–21.7%) in Group 2, and 27.5% (95% CI: 22.3%–33.3%) in Group 3.

### 3.3 Secondary Outcomes and Subgroup Robustness
Model 2 secondary associations were (**Figure 1B**):
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Dual High aOR 2.36 (95% CI: 1.70–3.28; RERI: 0.93, 95% CI: -0.16 to 2.01; multiplicative $p = 0.1102$), whereas isolated high hs-CRP had an aOR of 0.99 and isolated high VCTE had an aOR of 1.45.
- **Clinical Cardiovascular Disease:** Dual High aOR 1.50 (95% CI: 1.01–2.23; RERI: 0.59, 95% CI: 0.02–1.16; multiplicative $p = 0.0511$).
- **Very-High-Risk Chronic Kidney Disease (KDIGO Criteria):** Dual High aOR 1.36 (95% CI: 0.57–3.27; RERI: -0.10, multiplicative $p = 0.9342$, $n = 99$ events). The interval was wide.
- **Secondary Validation in Extended Adult Core Cohort ($N = 11,585$, aged $\ge 20$ years):** The CVD interval included the null:
  - Clinical Cardiovascular Disease: Dual High aOR 1.52 (95% CI: 0.99–2.33; RERI: 0.50, multiplicative $p = 0.0918$, $n = 9,757$ complete cases).
  - Chronic Kidney Disease: Dual High aOR 2.28 (95% CI: 1.71–3.04; RERI: 0.74, multiplicative $p = 0.0888$, $n = 9,721$ complete cases).

Subgroup estimates were:
- **Sex Stratification:** Men Dual High aOR 2.45 (95% CI: 1.45–4.15; RERI: 1.63, multiplicative $p = 0.0011$) versus Women aOR 1.71 (95% CI: 1.14–2.57; RERI: -0.15, multiplicative $p = 0.7072$).
- **Age Stratification (<60 vs. $\ge 60$):** Age <60 aOR 1.73 (95% CI: 0.93–3.21; RERI: -0.03) versus Age $\ge 60$ aOR 2.45 (95% CI: 1.55–3.88; RERI: 1.61, multiplicative $p = 0.0005$).
- **Age Stratification (<65 vs. $\ge 65$):** Age <65 aOR 1.89 (95% CI: 1.09–3.29; RERI: 0.40) versus Age $\ge 65$ aOR 2.78 (95% CI: 1.66–4.68; RERI: 1.98, multiplicative $p = 0.0097$).
- **Metabolic Status (Diabetes):** Diabetes aOR 1.75 (95% CI: 1.06–2.87; RERI: 1.16, multiplicative $p = 0.0188$) versus No Diabetes aOR 1.36 (95% CI: 0.81–2.29; RERI: 0.19, multiplicative $p = 0.4752$).
- **NHANES Survey Cycles:** 2017–March 2020 aOR 2.18 (95% CI: 1.41–3.38; RERI: 1.07) versus 2021–2023 aOR 1.80 (95% CI: 0.89–3.65; RERI: 0.71).
Positive interaction estimates were most apparent among men, older adults ($\ge 65$ years), and participants with diabetes; formal between-subgroup heterogeneity was not tested.

### 3.4 Sensitivity and Mechanistic Analyses
Sensitivity analyses showed:
1. **Steatosis vs. Fibrosis:** Replacing VCTE with CAP ($\ge 274$ dB/m), dual-high aOR was 1.60 (95% CI: 1.17–2.19; RERI: 0.10, multiplicative $p = 0.9073$), versus 2.01 for VCTE.
2. **Alternative hs-CRP Cutoffs:** At hs-CRP $\ge 3.0$ mg/L, dual-high aOR was 1.86 (95% CI: 1.18–2.93; RERI: 0.54, 95% CI: -0.30 to 1.38, multiplicative $p = 0.2224$).
3. **Unbridged hs-CRP:** Unbridged measurements yielded Dual High aOR 1.84 (95% CI: 1.26–2.69; RERI: 0.78, multiplicative $p = 0.0472$).
4. **Three-Level VCTE Severity:** Stiffness 8.0 to <12.0 kPa plus high hs-CRP yielded aOR 2.26 (95% CI: 1.50–3.40; $p = 0.0003$; product interaction $p = 0.034$). Stiffness $\ge 12.0$ kPa plus high hs-CRP yielded aOR 1.68 (95% CI: 1.03–2.74; $p = 0.0391$; product interaction $p = 0.125$). Cirrhotic-range stiffness showed attenuation rather than a monotonic gradient.
5. **Continuous Biomarker Scale:** Standardized log₂ hs-CRP had aOR 1.13 per SD (95% CI: 1.03–1.24, $p = 0.0125$); stiffness had aOR 1.02 (95% CI: 0.91–1.16, $p = 0.6982$); their product had aOR 1.08 (95% CI: 0.97–1.21, $p = 0.1553$).
6. **Exclusion of Acute Inflammation ($N = 6,557$):** With hs-CRP $\le 10.0$ mg/L, Dual High aOR was 1.92 (95% CI: 1.34–2.76; RERI: 0.88, multiplicative $p = 0.0106$).
7. **Symmetric PREVENT Input Domain Restriction ($N = 6,884$):** Within PREVENT input bounds, Dual High aOR was 2.12 (95% CI: 1.46–3.08; RERI: 0.89, multiplicative $p = 0.0481$).
8. **Flexible Non-Linear Age Adjustment ($N = 7,043$):** Cubic B-splines for age (df = 4) yielded Dual High aOR 2.03 (95% CI: 1.39–2.96; RERI: 0.87, multiplicative $p = 0.0341$); residual confounding remains possible.
9. **VCTE $\times$ Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index ($N = 7,043$):** Substituting SII $\ge 655$ for hs-CRP yielded Dual High aOR 1.86 (95% CI: 1.19–2.91; RERI: 0.35, multiplicative $p = 0.6742$); interaction attenuated, without testing biomarker specificity.

### 3.5 Head-to-Head Diagnostic Comparison: FIB-4 vs. Physical VCTE
FIB-4 $\ge 1.30$ was compared with VCTE $\ge 8.0$ kPa in 7,043 participants (**Table 3**, **Figure 2**).

**Diagnostic Test Accuracy Overall:**
- **Standard FIB-4 Cutoff ($\ge 1.30$):**
  - **Sensitivity:** 34.8% (65.2% of VCTE-positive participants missed).
  - **Specificity:** 75.8%
  - **Positive Predictive Value (PPV):** 14.5% (approximately 1 in 7 FIB-4-positive participants VCTE-positive).
  - **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):** 90.8%
  - **Positive Likelihood Ratio (+LR):** 1.44
  - **False-Positive Rate:** 24.2%
- **Age-Calibrated FIB-4 Strategy (Cutoff 1.30 for <65; Cutoff 2.00 for $\ge 65$):**
  - **Sensitivity:** 25.7%
  - **Specificity:** 85.1%
  - **Positive Predictive Value (PPV):** 16.8%
  - **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):** 90.7%
  - **Positive Likelihood Ratio (+LR):** 1.72
  - **False-Positive Rate:** 14.9%

**Severe Age-Driven Diagnostic Inaccuracy:**
Age-stratified performance differed:
- **Younger Adults (Ages 30–64, $N = 5,223$, cutoff $\ge 1.30$):** Specificity was 85.7%, and the false-positive rate was 14.3% (sensitivity: 24.6%, PPV: 15.8%, +LR: 1.72).
- **Older Adults (Ages 65–79, $N = 1,820$, standard cutoff $\ge 1.30$):** Specificity was 36.2%, and the false-positive rate **surged 4.5-fold to 63.8%** (**Figure 2A**; sensitivity: 64.6%, PPV: 13.2%, +LR: 1.01).
- **Older Adults with Age-Calibrated Cutoff ($\ge 2.00$, $N = 1,820$):** Specificity was 82.5% and reduced the false-positive rate from 63.8% down to 17.5% (sensitivity: 29.0%, PPV: 19.9%, +LR: 1.65).

**Discrimination for Advanced CKM Syndrome:**
Apparent AUCs used delete-1-PSU jackknife CIs ($df = 40$), conditional on fixed full-sample predictions, without out-of-sample validation (**Table 3**, **Figure 2B**):
- **Base Model 2 (Demographics + Socioeconomics + Adiposity):** AUC 0.8636 (95% CI: 0.8495–0.8777)
- **Model 2 + Continuous VCTE Stiffness:** AUC 0.8636 (95% CI: 0.8494–0.8777)
- **Model 2 + Continuous FIB-4:** AUC 0.8638 (95% CI: 0.8496–0.8780)
- **Model 2 + Dual VCTE and hs-CRP:** AUC 0.8643 (95% CI: 0.8503–0.8783)
- **Model 2 + Dual FIB-4 and SII:** AUC 0.8652 (95% CI: 0.8512–0.8792)

VCTE versus FIB-4 showed $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.0002$ (95% CI: -0.0013 to 0.0009). Dual VCTE/hs-CRP versus Base Model 2 showed $\Delta\text{AUC}$ of $+0.0007$ (95% CI: -0.0007 to 0.0021).

## 4. DISCUSSION

### 4.1 Principal Findings and Mechanistic Insights
VCTE ≥8.0 kPa and hs-CRP ≥2.0 mg/L showed multiplicative interaction ($p = 0.0185$). Model 2 odds-scale RERI was 0.91 (95% CI: 0.19–1.63), AP 0.45 (95% CI: 0.20–0.70); standardized prevalence interaction was +5.9 percentage points (95% CI: 1.3–10.5), $\text{RERI}_{\text{PR}}$ 0.47 (95% CI: 0.12–0.83), and $\text{AP}_{\text{PR}}$ 31.0% (95% CI: 10.4–51.7%). These contrasts reflect statistical interaction, not causal fractions. Isolated stiffness had aOR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.53–1.35), versus 2.01 for co-elevation.

FIB-4 and VCTE models had similar discrimination (AUC approximately 0.864). Standard FIB-4 FPR was 4.5-fold higher at age ≥65 (63.8% versus 14.3%), without isolating age's causal contribution or establishing age-independent VCTE accuracy.

### 4.2 Biological Plausibility of Multi-Organ Crosstalk
Hepatic stellate-cell activation, inflammatory cytokines, endothelial dysfunction, and renal injury link liver disease with CVD and CKD [8,16,23–27]. The dual-high phenotype exhibited the strongest combined point-estimate association with chronic kidney disease (aOR: 2.36, 95% CI: 1.70–3.28), alongside positive albeit imprecisely estimated additive interaction (RERI: 0.93, 95% CI: -0.16 to 2.01).

### 4.3 Clinical and Policy Implications
1. **FIB-4 Screening:** Relative to VCTE, standard FIB-4 specificity was 36.2% and FPR 63.8% at age ≥65. Age calibration (≥2.0) reduces false positives while sacrificing sensitivity; screening consequences require evaluation [19,28].
2. **Dual-Biomarker Phenotypic Stratification:** Although adding continuous liver stiffness and hs-CRP to demographic and adiposity covariates did not materially increase overall C-statistics in the general population ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.0007$), categorical co-elevation of VCTE ($\ge 8.0$ kPa) and hs-CRP ($\ge 2.0$ mg/L) identified a high-risk clinical phenotype with a 5.9-percentage-point excess standardized prevalence of advanced CKM syndrome. Prospective studies are warranted to evaluate whether this fibro-inflammatory phenotype derives preferential benefit from multi-target cardiometabolic therapies—such as glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs), dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), and non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists [29,30].

### 4.4 Strengths and Limitations
**Full-Universe Design-Based Variance Estimation** retained 27,493 participants for survey variance (df = 40). Standardized VCTE measurements avoid formula overlap, and CDC assay bridges address analyzer transitions.

Cross-sectional data cannot establish causality or incidence. Passive hepatic venous congestion in right heart failure can elevate VCTE stiffness independently of fibrosis; clinical CVD was not excluded. VCTE was not validated against biopsy here. Interaction attenuated on continuous scales; subgroup concentration does not establish between-subgroup heterogeneity. Model 3 adjusted for antihypertensive and hypoglycemic, but not lipid-lowering, therapy.

## 5. CONCLUSIONS

Co-elevated VCTE ≥8.0 kPa and hs-CRP ≥2.0 mg/L doubled advanced CKM odds: $\text{RERI}_{\text{OR}}$ 0.91, $\text{AP}_{\text{OR}}$ 0.45, standardized prevalence interaction +5.9 percentage points, and $\text{AP}_{\text{PR}}$ 31.0%. These contrasts are not causal fractions. FIB-4 FPR was 63.8% at age ≥65 relative to VCTE, an unvalidated biopsy surrogate here. Prospective validation and clinical utility studies are needed before changing screening or treatment pathways.
